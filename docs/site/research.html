METHOD AND APPLICATION

Research context

Morpheus analyses in corpus-based study of Ancient Greek.

Nestle1904 Text-Fabric

The initial Morphkit release was developed alongside the Nestle1904 Greek New Testament Text-Fabric dataset. Its Greek processing can assign SP-style tags following the conventions used in this corpus. This makes it possible to compare Morpheus candidate analyses with pre-existing corpus annotations, inspect points of agreement or difference, and support further review.

The tag conversion is project-oriented: it maps Morpheus features into an established annotation scheme. It is not a claim that the SP scheme is inherent to Morpheus or that the conversion resolves the linguistic analysis.

Ranking and ambiguity

Greek word forms can have multiple plausible analyses. Morphkit can decode tags into features and calculate a weighted similarity between tags. Its sorting utility can prioritize a reference lemma and order analyses using tag similarity. As the notebook cautions, this score is a practical ranking aid; it is not a probability, a contextual parser, or a measure of syntactic fit.

Limits of word-level analysis

Part-of-speech assignment in this release uses rule-based heuristics over the features present in each parse. A word-level morphological analyser does not consider the full sentence context. Some forms may remain ambiguous, and corpus annotations may differ because of context, editorial decisions, or schema differences. Such cases call for philological inspection rather than automatic acceptance of a top-ranked candidate.

Latin

The usage notebook also demonstrates basic Latin support for retrieving, splitting, and parsing Morpheus blocks. The richer POS and SP-tag workflow documented here is oriented toward Greek.
